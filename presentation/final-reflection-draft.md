# Final reflection (draft, about 800 words; the course asks for 500–800)

*Draft for the Teams channel. Each team member should read it, correct it and add their own view before posting. Numbers are from our tracker, Search Console and recorded screenshots up to 18 Nov 2026; refresh them on 25 Nov before posting.*

## What we set out to do

We were given an invented word, "Grohnpetrel", and asked to win Google visibility for it. We decided that the word should mean something we could support with real sources: a fictional coastal-conservation and seabird-education project from Bremen-Grohn. The name already contains a real place (Grohn) and a real bird family (petrels), so every page could connect the invented word to checkable facts about North Sea seabirds. We built the site from a public GitHub repository that publishes to WordPress, tracked Google and Brave every day in Germany, the US and the UK, and wrote one log entry for every change. From 16 November our main metric was our Google rank for the bare query "grohnpetrel".

## Successful approaches, with evidence

**Fixing indexing first.** On day one the site sent a hidden `noindex` header. We found it by checking the HTTP response, not the WordPress settings. After the fix and the first publish on 31 October we ranked #2 to #4 straight away.

**A second language.** We published a German version on 3 November. On 4 November we were #1 on Google Germany for "grohnpetrel", after not being in the top 10 the day before. Germany stayed our strongest market: in the top 10 on 15 of 19 tracked days, and the lecturer's snapshot showed us #1 on 9 November.

**Citable sources for AI answers.** We archived the project on Zenodo with a DOI and kept the repository public. Brave showed our Zenodo record as the top result for "what is grohnpetrel" in all three countries from 11 November, and on 18 November Google's AI Overview listed our meaning first and cited our repository. One quotable sentence, structured data and an archived source made us findable for engines that look for sources.

**Exact-match stability.** For the quoted query "grohnpetrel" we were in the UK top 10 on all 19 days and #1 on 11 of them.

**One canonical page.** On 16 November we merged our "What is Grohnpetrel?" and "meanings" pages into one page, /about-grohnpetrel/, with redirects. Two days later we were #1 on Google US for the bare query, for the first time since 2 November. This is early evidence, not proof: Google still showed the old address.

## Unsuccessful approaches, and why

**More text did not help.** On 4 November we made the definition, FAQ and front page two to three times longer. The next day we were out of the top 10 everywhere. Other teams' pages were indexed at the same time, so we cannot prove cause, but length was not what Google was missing.

**Too many pages for one query.** Between 14 and 16 November we added coastal-habitat pages, species profiles, a learning game and a Wadden Sea feature. They brought real seabird searches (for example 193 impressions for "atlantic puffin"), but over the project Google showed nine different pages of ours for the bare query, and our rank became unstable. Topic breadth helped reach; it did not help the brand query.

**Social media came too late.** By 7 November about half of the top 10 were Instagram posts and YouTube videos from other teams. We posted nothing until 11 November. The teams that led Germany and the UK in the final week also had video and posts in the results.

**Describing other teams.** On 9 November we published a page explaining the word's other meanings. On 10 November we lost #1 in Germany. It is a hypothesis, but placing other teams' topics next to our keyword may have helped them more than us. Ironically, Google's AI Overview later used exactly that page to explain the word: SEO and GEO can pull in different directions.

**Too many changes at once.** On some days we changed several things while other teams changed theirs, so we could not always tell what caused a movement.

## Lessons learned

- Indexing and a clean technical base matter more than content length in the first week.
- For a new word, the results page is shared with social video; a website alone is not enough.
- One page per search intent: our own pages must not compete for the main query.
- Say clearly what you are; never describe what others are.
- AI answers cite sources they can find: a public repository, an archive with a DOI and one clear sentence.
- Some search appearance is not ours to control: Google shows the university's name and icon for every team site.

## What we would do differently

We would choose one canonical page on day one, post the first short video in the first week, write the multilingual plan at the start, seek a few real links from university and course pages, and agree a "no edits" window before each measurement, changing one thing at a time and waiting 48 hours. We would also keep competitor analysis entirely private from the start.

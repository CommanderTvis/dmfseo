In brief: The common tern (Sterna hirundo) is a slender, fork-tailed seabird that nests on beaches, dunes, salt marshes and islands, and spends the winter as far away as western and southern Africa.

Key facts

How it lives

Common terns catch small fish by plunge-diving from 1–6 m above the water. They breed in colonies, usually of fewer than 2,000 pairs, on flat ground with little vegetation; they also accept floating rafts built for them.

Eggs hatch after 21–22 days, and the chicks fly at 22–28 days old. European birds then migrate south and spend the winter mostly off western or southern Africa.

Main threats

  • Disturbance of colonies by people, dogs, vehicles and boats.
  • Flooding of low-lying nests.
  • Predators such as rats and large gulls, and gulls taking over nest sites.
  • Loss of nesting habitat and pollution.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.

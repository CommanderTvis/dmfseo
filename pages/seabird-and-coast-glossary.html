In brief: Short, plain-language definitions of the terms used on this website, from “bird cliff” to “Wadden Sea”. Each term links to the page where we explain it in more detail.

Grohnpetrel and the project

Grohnpetrel

A fictional coastal-conservation and seabird-education initiative from Bremen-Grohn, Germany, created in 2026 by the Guillemots student team at Constructor University. Not a bird species and not a registered organisation. Full definition.

Guillemots (team)

The four-student team behind Grohnpetrel. Its name is the English name of a group of seabirds that includes the common guillemot. About the team.

Birds

Seabird

A bird that finds most of its food at sea, such as gannets, puffins, kittiwakes, terns and petrels. Seabirds of the North Sea.

Petrel

A seabird of the tube-nosed group (order Procellariiformes), which spends most of its life on the open sea. The word is the second half of “Grohnpetrel”. European storm petrel.

Northern fulmar

A seabird of the petrel family that feeds at the sea surface. Because it swallows floating plastic, North Sea countries use it to measure plastic pollution. Plastic and North Sea seabirds.

Northern gannet

One of the largest seabirds of the North Atlantic, which dives head-first for fish. In Germany it breeds only on Helgoland. Northern gannet.

Common guillemot

An auk (Uria aalge) that breeds on narrow cliff ledges. On Helgoland about 2,000 to 2,500 pairs breed each year, and the Lummenfelsen cliff is named after it (“Lumme” is German for guillemot); its chicks jump from the cliff into the sea before they can fly (NABU).

Auk

A family of diving seabirds (Alcidae) that “fly” underwater with their wings, including guillemots and the Atlantic puffin.

Places and habitats

Bird cliff

A steep rock face where seabirds breed close together on ledges. The Lummenfelsen on Helgoland is Germany’s only bird cliff.

Colony

A place where many birds of one or several species breed close together, such as the gannet colony on Helgoland.

Wadden Sea

The coastal sea of the Netherlands, Germany and Denmark, the largest unbroken system of intertidal sand and mud flats in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2009 (UNESCO). Why the Wadden Sea matters.

Tidal flat

Sand or mud that the sea covers at high tide and uncovers at low tide; rich in worms and shellfish that many birds eat.

Salt marsh

Coastal grassland that is flooded by the sea from time to time; a breeding and resting place for birds.

Flyway

The route a population of migratory birds uses between breeding and wintering areas. The Wadden Sea lies on the East Atlantic Flyway.

Threats and monitoring

Marine litter

Waste that ends up in the sea or on beaches, much of it plastic. Seabird conservation threats.

Microplastics

Plastic pieces less than five millimetres long (NOAA).

Ghost nets

Fishing nets and lines that are lost or abandoned at sea and keep trapping animals.

Bycatch

Animals caught unintentionally in fishing gear, for example seabirds diving after bait.

OSPAR

The convention for the protection of the marine environment of the North-East Atlantic, under which North Sea countries monitor pollution together.

Fulmar plastic indicator

The share of beached fulmars with more than 0.1 g of plastic in their stomachs. The target is at most 10%; on the German North Sea coast it was 48% in 2019–2023 (German Environment Agency).

IUCN Red List

The global list that assesses how threatened species are, from “Least Concern” to “Extinct” (IUCN Red List).

The gannet: the North Sea’s plunge diver (45 seconds)

Video transcript

The gannet: the North Sea’s plunge diver · in 45 seconds

Northern gannet · Morus bassanus · 87–100 cm · Photo: Carsten Steger, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (cropped)

Wingspan · 1.8 m · wings up to 1.8 m across: one of the biggest seabirds of the North Atlantic. · Source: Wikipedia (northern gannet)

How it hunts · It hits the water at up to about 100 km/h. · Momentum carries it about 11 m down; then it swims on, on average to about 20 m. · Source: Wikipedia (northern gannet)

Helgoland · since 1991 · gannets breed again on Germany’s only bird cliff: about 680 pairs in 2016. · Source: Wikipedia (northern gannet)

Bird flu, 2022 · −71 % · occupied nest sites at Bass Rock, the largest colony, after the H5N1 outbreak. · Source: BirdLife International

How you can help · Keep your distance from nesting birds · Keep dogs on a lead near colonies · Take your rubbish home

The Atlantic puffin in 40 seconds

Video transcript

The Atlantic puffin · in 40 seconds

Atlantic puffin · Fratercula arctica · 28–30 cm · Photo: Charles J. Sharp, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons (cropped)

A life at sea · Puffins spend autumn and winter far out on the open ocean. · They come to the coast only in late spring to breed. Young birds stay at sea for two to three years. · Source: Wikipedia (Atlantic puffin)

Every day · about 40 · small fish for one adult puffin, so colonies need plenty of food nearby. · Source: Wikipedia (Atlantic puffin)

One burrow · 1 egg · per pair, laid in a burrow on a grassy cliff top. · Source: Wikipedia (Atlantic puffin)

IUCN Red List · Vulnerable since 2015. · Threats: fewer sandeels, climate change, rats and cats on nesting islands, oil and fishing nets. · Source: Wikipedia (Atlantic puffin), IUCN

Dunes, salt marshes, beaches: three coastal habitats in 35 seconds

Video transcript

Dunes, salt marshes, beaches · three coastal habitats in 35 seconds

Three habitats · Dunes · held together by marram grass · Shelduck and curlew breed here; a dune chain can replace a dike. · Salt marshes · flooded up to twice a day · They shield dikes from storm surges; oystercatcher and redshank breed here. · Open beaches · nests in a scrape on bare sand · Little terns and plovers lay almost invisible eggs.

Walkers with dogs, in the dunes · 93.8 % · of approaches flushed incubating Kentish plovers from their nests (47.6 % without dogs). · Source: Gómez-Serrano 2020, as cited on our page

April to July · The main breeding season of beach-nesting birds. · Lower Saxony Wadden Sea National Park: in bird areas, stay on marked paths from 1 April to 31 July. · Source: Lower Saxony Wadden Sea National Park

Visit responsibly · Use marked paths and boardwalks · Keep dogs on a lead · Stay outside fenced breeding areas

Is there a bird called Grohnpetrel?

Video transcript

Is there a bird called Grohnpetrel?

The short answer · No. · Grohnpetrel is the name of a fictional student project, not a bird species.

Where the name comes from · a district of Bremen-Vegesack on the river Weser · Grohn · + · tube-nosed seabirds that live on the open sea · petrel

Meet a real petrel · The European storm petrel · 14–18 cm long: one of Europe’s smallest seabirds. It nests in crevices and burrows on remote islands. · Source: Wikipedia (European storm petrel)

At night only · It visits its colony only after dark. · At sea it flutters low over the waves and can find oily food by smell. · Source: Wikipedia (European storm petrel)

Plastic and North Sea seabirds: four numbers

Video transcript

Plastic and North Sea seabirds · four numbers

Northern fulmars, german north sea coast, 2019–2023 · 48% · had more than 0.1 g of plastic in their stomachs. · Measured: 48% · International target: at most 10% · Source: German Environment Agency (UBA)

Same birds, any plastic · 89% · of 93 fulmars examined had some plastic in their stomachs. · Source: German Environment Agency (UBA)

Gannet nests, 29 colonies · 46% · of 7,280 nests contained debris such as fishing rope and nets. · Source: O’Hanlon et al. 2019

Prediction for 2050 · 99% · of seabird species may be swallowing plastic. · Source: Wilcox et al. 2015

What you can do · Take all your rubbish home · Collect lost fishing line and net pieces · Join a recognised beach clean

The Wadden Sea in numbers

Video transcript

The Wadden Sea in numbers

Unesco world heritage since 2009 · The largest unbroken system of tidal sand and mud flats in the world. · Shared by the Netherlands, Germany and Denmark. · Source: UNESCO

Every year · 10–12 million · migratory birds pass through the Wadden Sea. · Source: UNESCO

At the same time · 6.1 million · birds can be there at once. · Source: UNESCO

Long-term trends · Increasing: Eurasian spoonbill, barnacle goose · Declining: common eider, pied avocet · Declining: Eurasian oystercatcher · Source: Trilateral Wadden Sea waterbird report, 2025

4 simple ways to help seabirds

Video transcript

4 simple ways to help seabirds

Step 1 of 4 · Reduce plastic waste. · Reusable bottles, less single-use plastic, rubbish in the bin.

Step 2 of 4 · Keep your distance from nesting birds. · Quiet, from afar, dogs on a lead near colonies.

Step 3 of 4 · Do not feed wild seabirds. · Our food can harm them and changes their behaviour.

Step 4 of 4 · Learn and share reliable information. · Facts with sources help more than slogans.

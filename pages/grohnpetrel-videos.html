Is there a bird called Grohnpetrel?

Video transcript

Is there a bird called Grohnpetrel?

The short answer · No. · Grohnpetrel is the name of a fictional student project, not a bird species.

Where the name comes from · a district of Bremen-Vegesack on the river Weser · Grohn · + · tube-nosed seabirds that live on the open sea · petrel

Meet a real petrel · The European storm petrel · 14–18 cm long: one of Europe’s smallest seabirds. It nests in crevices and burrows on remote islands. · Source: Wikipedia (European storm petrel)

At night only · It visits its colony only after dark. · At sea it flutters low over the waves and can find oily food by smell. · Source: Wikipedia (European storm petrel)

Plastic and North Sea seabirds: four numbers

Video transcript

Plastic and North Sea seabirds · four numbers

Northern fulmars, german north sea coast, 2019–2023 · 48% · had more than 0.1 g of plastic in their stomachs. · Measured: 48% · International target: at most 10% · Source: German Environment Agency (UBA)

Same birds, any plastic · 89% · of 93 fulmars examined had some plastic in their stomachs. · Source: German Environment Agency (UBA)

Gannet nests, 29 colonies · 46% · of 7,280 nests contained debris such as fishing rope and nets. · Source: O’Hanlon et al. 2019

Prediction for 2050 · 99% · of seabird species may be swallowing plastic. · Source: Wilcox et al. 2015

What you can do · Take all your rubbish home · Collect lost fishing line and net pieces · Join a recognised beach clean

The Wadden Sea in numbers

Video transcript

The Wadden Sea in numbers

Unesco world heritage since 2009 · The largest unbroken system of tidal sand and mud flats in the world. · Shared by the Netherlands, Germany and Denmark. · Source: UNESCO

Every year · 10–12 million · migratory birds pass through the Wadden Sea. · Source: UNESCO

At the same time · 6.1 million · birds can be there at once. · Source: UNESCO

Long-term trends · Increasing: Eurasian spoonbill, barnacle goose · Declining: common eider, pied avocet · Declining: Eurasian oystercatcher · Source: Trilateral Wadden Sea waterbird report, 2025

4 simple ways to help seabirds

Video transcript

4 simple ways to help seabirds

Step 1 of 4 · Reduce plastic waste. · Reusable bottles, less single-use plastic, rubbish in the bin.

Step 2 of 4 · Keep your distance from nesting birds. · Quiet, from afar, dogs on a lead near colonies.

Step 3 of 4 · Do not feed wild seabirds. · Our food can harm them and changes their behaviour.

Step 4 of 4 · Learn and share reliable information. · Facts with sources help more than slogans.

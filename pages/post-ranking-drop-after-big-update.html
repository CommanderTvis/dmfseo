In brief: On 3 November our site dropped out of Google’s top 10 for the bare search “grohnpetrel” in Germany, the US and the UK, a day after our biggest update. Another course team’s site held positions 1 to 4. We still ranked for several longer searches. This post records what happened, our explanations and what we changed in response.

What the numbers show

Figures come from our daily, non-personalised rank check from data-centre locations; personal searches can differ. The full history is on our rank dashboard.

What we had changed just before

On 2 and 3 November we published our largest update so far: About, Contact and Privacy pages, structured data on every page, new page titles, a six-species seabird guide, this Journal and a full German version of the site. Large changes mean Google has to re-crawl and re-evaluate many pages at once.

Our explanations

  • A rival site with depth on the term itself. The site at the top answered “what is Grohnpetrel” with long, focused pages in two languages, while our definition page had about 220 words and our FAQ eight short answers.
  • Re-evaluation after a big update. Rankings often move while search engines process many changed pages; one day of data is not a trend.
  • Language. Our German pages only went live on 3 November; if German is part of the reason, our ranks in Germany should recover first after the next crawl.

What we changed on 4 November

  • Expanded What is Grohnpetrel? to about 580 words with an at-a-glance table, what Grohnpetrel is not and a one-sentence description to quote.
  • Expanded the FAQ from 8 to 14 questions, including why other websites use the same invented word.
  • Added the Grohnpetrel mascot with descriptive alt text to these pages, and made it the image shown when our pages are shared.
  • Made the same changes on the German pages.

How we will judge it

If our definition and FAQ pages return to the top 10 for “grohnpetrel” and “what is grohnpetrel” within the next crawls, the depth explanation gains support; if Germany recovers first, the language one does. We will add the results to our SEO / GEO Lab, including if nothing changes.

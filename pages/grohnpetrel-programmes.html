Grohnpetrel offers three educational programs aimed at promoting knowledge about coastal ecosystems and the protection of seabirds.

Each program is designed to address a different aspect of environmental conservation, ranging from classroom learning to community involvement and encouraging responsible behavior.

Although these activities are not being carried out yet, they represent some of the initiatives we would like to develop in the future.

Our proposed programs

1. Coastal Classroom

Coastal Classroom is an educational program designed to bring knowledge about coastal ecosystems to students of different ages.

Its main idea is to show that learning about the environment doesn’t have to be limited to textbooks. The program would include interactive workshops, presentations, visual materials, and educational activities where participants could learn about different seabird species, understand their importance, and discover the environmental issues they face.

We would also look to collaborate with schools and universities interested in incorporating coastal conservation topics into their educational activities.

2. Citizen Observation

Citizen Observation is a proposal that aims to encourage people to get to know seabirds better through responsible observation in nature. The goal would be to spark interest in the species that live along the coasts and promote a greater appreciation of their ecosystems.

Proposed activities would include educational tours in authorized coastal areas, bird identification sessions, and basic guides for learning how to watch them without disturbing their natural behavior.

Participants could also learn how to record their observations and share them through existing citizen science platforms, following their guidelines.

3. Responsible Coast

Responsible Coast would be focused on promoting habits that help reduce human impact on beaches and coastal ecosystems.

This program would propose educational campaigns about waste reduction, responsible consumption, and the importance of respecting protected natural areas. It would also include informational materials and awareness activities aimed at students and visitors in coastal areas.

Our goal would be to show that everyday decisions can affect the state of ecosystems and that community involvement is important for their conservation.

How Would We Measure Our Success?

To evaluate the success of our programs, Grohnpetrel proposes using different indicators that allow us to understand their reach and effectiveness. These would include the number of people participating in activities, the level of knowledge participants have gained, and their opinions on the educational materials.

We could also use short surveys before and after workshops to gauge how much participants have learned. In the case of information campaigns, we would analyze the reach of the posts and the audience's interaction with our content.

These indicators would help us identify which activities are most useful and what aspects we could improve in the future.

In brief: The Eurasian oystercatcher (Haematopus ostralegus) is a black-and-white shorebird with a long orange-red bill, common on European coasts and mudflats. Despite its name, oysters are not a big part of its diet. It is classed as Near Threatened.

Key facts

How it lives

Oystercatchers usually pair for many years and start breeding at three to five years old. Both parents incubate the eggs for 24–27 days, and the chicks leave the nest a day or two after hatching, still fed by their parents.

Because they depend on shellfish and worms in healthy mudflats, oystercatchers are used as an indicator of the state of coastal ecosystems; long-term studies of their feeding have been carried out in northern Germany and the Netherlands.

Main threats

  • Disturbance near nests on open ground, where eggs and chicks are well camouflaged and easy to step on.
  • For the wider threats to coastal birds, see Seabird Conservation: Threats and Challenges.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.

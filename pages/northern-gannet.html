In brief: The northern gannet (Morus bassanus) is one of the biggest seabirds of the North Atlantic, with wings up to 1.8 m across. It catches fish by diving head-first into the sea from a great height, and since 1991 it has bred again on Helgoland, Germany’s only bird cliff.

Key facts

How it lives

Gannets hunt by plunge-diving: they fold their wings and hit the water at up to about 100 km/h, reach roughly 11 m by momentum, then swim deeper, on average to about 20 m. Fish that shoal near the surface make up most of their diet.

They nest in dense colonies on cliffs and rocky islands. The biggest colonies are in Scotland, Ireland, Wales, Iceland and Canada; BirdLife International puts the world population at 1.5–1.8 million mature birds. Gannets can live for more than 20 years.

Main threats

  • Bird flu: in 2022 the H5N1 avian influenza caused unusually high deaths at about 40 colonies, roughly three quarters of all known colonies. At Bass Rock, occupied nest sites fell by at least 71%.
  • Fishing gear: birds are caught as bycatch.
  • Wind farms: collisions with turbines at sea.
  • Waste and plastic pollution in the sea.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.

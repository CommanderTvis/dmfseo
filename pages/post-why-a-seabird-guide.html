In brief: We added a guide to six real seabirds of the North Sea region because a website about an invented word needs real, useful content to earn trust. Every fact on those pages links to its source.

The problem with an invented keyword

Several course teams compete for the same made-up word, “Grohnpetrel”. Search engines and AI assistants have no prior knowledge of it, so they look for signals that a site is a reliable source: clear authorship, consistent facts and content that helps people.

What we built

The Seabirds of the North Sea guide covers the northern gannet, Atlantic puffin, black-legged kittiwake, common tern, Eurasian oystercatcher and European storm petrel. Each page answers the obvious questions first (size, food, where it breeds, global status), then explains the main threats.

How we checked the facts

  • We took figures only from the sources linked at the end of each page and give ranges where sources differ.
  • We wrote everything in our own words and dated the check (2 November 2026).
  • We kept the fictional and the real apart: Grohnpetrel is our invented concept; the birds and their facts are real.

What we expect

We expect the guide pages to bring in searches about the birds themselves and to make our site look more credible to AI assistants that answer questions about Grohnpetrel. We will record the results in our SEO / GEO Lab, including if nothing changes.

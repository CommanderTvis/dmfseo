How the Guillemots team measures whether an invented term, Grohnpetrel, becomes discoverable in search engines and AI assistants, and the evidence we have collected so far.

Research status: this page reports only observations we have actually made, with dates. We do not claim rankings, impressions, backlinks or AI citations that we have not measured.

Research question

Can a new, invented term such as “Grohnpetrel” become findable, correctly understood and cited across traditional search (SEO), answer engines (AEO) and generative AI assistants (GEO), and which actions make a measurable difference?

What we measure

Fixed test prompts

  • Grohnpetrel
  • What is Grohnpetrel?
  • Is Grohnpetrel a real bird species?
  • What is the Grohnpetrel initiative?
  • What does Grohnpetrel aim to achieve?
  • Who created Grohnpetrel?

Method: hypothesis, action, observation

  1. Record a baseline before publishing anything.
  2. Publish useful core pages: a clear definition, mission, programmes, sourced guides and FAQ, with internal links.
  3. Check technical discovery: response headers, sitemap, indexability and Search Console.
  4. Test controlled changes and note the dates.
  5. Compare and reflect: separate measured changes from interpretations, and compare fairly with other teams.

Baseline evidence (before publication)

Before any page existed, all three search engines and all five AI answer tools we tested either did not recognise “Grohnpetrel” or treated it as a misspelling of real seabird names such as the giant petrel or grey petrel.

Technical finding: the server told search engines not to index

On 28 October 2026, every URL we tested on business.constructor.university returned the header X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow. According to Google’s documentation, this keeps pages out of search results. The header is set on the server, outside WordPress. Status: resolved; by 31 October Google was ranking our pages, and on 1 November our own check confirmed the header was gone. More in our Journal article What a Hidden Noindex Header Taught Us About SEO.

Results so far: Google rankings

On 31 October and 1 November only our front page ranked. Since then Google has changed which of our pages it shows: on 7 November it was our German definition page in Germany, and the English front page or definition page in the US and UK. The quoted query has been #1 in the US and UK on most days since 1 November. For the bare query, positions change from day to day, and on several days Google showed no student team’s site at all in the US and UK. Brave and DuckDuckGo have not shown any page of our website in the top 10, but since 4 November Brave has ranked our Zenodo record in its top 4 on most days for “grohnpetrel”, the quoted query and “what is grohnpetrel”.

Data report: how an invented word appeared in search, 31 October–11 November 2026

This section reports what our own rank tracker recorded for “grohnpetrel” in Google during the first twelve days after publication. Every number below is derived from the tracker’s raw data with a published script, so anyone can recompute it. It describes observations, not causes.

Method

  • Source: our daily rank tracker. It queries Google through the Serper.dev API (interface language English; country Germany, the United States or the United Kingdom; top 10 organic results only) from a data-centre server, so results are not personalised and not tied to a city.
  • Queries: grohnpetrel, “grohnpetrel” (in quotes), what is grohnpetrel and grohnpetrel seabird. This report shows the first two.
  • Checks: several runs a day on 31 October and 1 November, then one run a day (12 checks: 4 queries × 3 countries). The table uses the last check of each day, as our public dashboard does.
  • Rank: the position of the highest-ranking page of our site among the organic results. “–” means none of our pages was in the results the API returned (sometimes fewer than 10). AI Overviews, videos and image results are not counted.

What the data shows

  • Quoted query: our site was in the top 10 on 11 of 12 days in Germany and the US and on all 12 days in the UK. It was #1 on 9 days in the UK, 8 in the US and 3 in Germany.
  • Bare query: much less stable. Our site was in the top 10 on 10 of 12 days in Germany (#1 on 4, 8 and 9 November), but only on 5 days in the US and 4 in the UK.
  • Germany vs. other countries: since 6 November our best result in Germany has been between #1 and #4 every day, mostly with the German definition page; in the UK the bare query has shown none of our pages since 9 November.
  • Short gaps: in Germany the bare query showed none of our pages on 3 and 5 November, while in the 9 days from 3 November on it showed our site on only 2 days in the US and 1 day in the UK.

How this differs from the course snapshots

Our lecturer records one screenshot a day in Google.de (English, desktop, Bremen). For the bare query it showed our site at #1 on 9 November, #3 on 10 November and #2 on 11 November; our tracker showed #1, #2 and #2 for Germany on the same days. The two methods use different locations, times of day and result features, so we report them separately and do not average them.

Possible explanations (hypotheses, not findings)

  • H1. Ambiguity: several student teams publish different meanings of the same new word, so Google may still be testing which sites to show for the bare query. This would predict continued day-to-day changes until the results settle; stable positions over a week would weaken it.
  • H2. Exact match: the quoted query is stable because only pages that contain the exact word qualify, and ours have many. A drop for the quoted query while the bare query stays stable would contradict this.
  • H3. Site changes: the gaps on 3 and 5 November fell on days with large changes to our site (the German version on 3 November, the redesign on 5 November). Two coinciding days cannot show cause; a repeated pattern after future large changes would make it more likely.
  • H4. Other formats: on some days the top result for the bare query in the UK and US was a social video or post, not a website, which our tracker does not count. This would mean organic rank alone understates how the word is presented.

Limitations

  • Twelve days, one API, one interface language and three countries; no search-volume data.
  • Real users see personalised and localised results, plus AI Overviews and videos, which this method leaves out.
  • The table shows our best page only, not which page or how many of our pages ranked.
  • We cannot see other teams’ changes in detail, so changes in our position may come from their work, from Google’s crawling or from updates, not only from ours.

7 November: what else is on the results page

Observed (7 November 2026, Google in English for Germany and the US, a manual check and our daily rank check):

  • For “grohnpetrel”, around half of the top 10 were not websites but social media: Instagram posts and reels, a YouTube channel and a YouTube Short, all about other student teams’ Grohnpetrel concepts. We have no posts or videos yet.
  • Google’s AI Overview described Grohnpetrel as a student brand of drinks and outdoor clothing. It did not mention a coastal-conservation concept and did not link to our pages.
  • For “what is grohnpetrel”, Google sometimes treats the word as a misspelling and shows unrelated pages, so this query is unstable for every team.
  • Search Console showed our automatic “Uncategorized” category page getting impressions for “grohnpetrel” on 2 November. It only repeats the Journal, so on 7 November we set category, tag and author pages to “noindex” and removed them from the sitemap.

Hypothesis: for a new word, search engines and AI answers describe whatever the most sources, in the most formats, say it is. Three of the concepts competing for this word are product brands, so the summary describes a brand. Next test: one short, clearly labelled video and one post about our project on team-owned channels, each linking to this website, then the same queries for 14 days. What would prove us wrong: no change in our positions or in the AI Overview within 14 days of the posts being indexed.

What we changed, and when

Each change is also recorded with its outcome in our project log. We will add dated results for each change here, including changes that show no effect.

What we expect, and what would prove us wrong

  • Hypothesis 1: a dedicated, answer-first definition page will be the page Google shows for “What is Grohnpetrel?”.
  • Hypothesis 2: clear, consistent facts across our pages and in structured data (who, what, where, status) will reduce the “misspelling” interpretations in AI answers.
  • Hypothesis 3: being indexed by Bing will matter for ChatGPT and Copilot visibility, not only Google indexing.

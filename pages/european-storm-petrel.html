In brief: The European storm petrel (Hydrobates pelagicus) is one of Europe’s smallest seabirds, just 14–18 cm long. It nests in crevices and burrows on remote islands and only visits its colony at night. It belongs to the petrels, the bird group in the name Grohnpetrel.

Key facts

How it lives

Storm petrels flutter low over the waves and pick food from the surface. They can find oily food by smell, and they often follow ships.

For a bird this small, they live remarkably long: ringing records show one storm petrel aged almost 32 years and another more than 33 years old.

Where the word “petrel” comes from

The word was first recorded in 1602 as a corruption of “pitteral”, describing how the birds patter across the water. The popular story that it refers to Saint Peter walking on the waves was invented later.

Main threats

  • Rats and other introduced mammals: storm petrels cannot survive on islands where rats live.
  • Cats and mink, which can reach islands close to the mainland.
  • Natural predators such as skuas, large gulls and owls.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.

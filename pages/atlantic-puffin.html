In brief: The Atlantic puffin (Fratercula arctica) is a small seabird of the North Atlantic that nests in burrows on grassy cliff tops. It has been classed as Vulnerable since 2015 because its numbers are falling fast in Europe.

Key facts

How it lives

Puffins spend autumn and winter far out on the open ocean and only come to the coast in late spring to breed. Young birds stay at sea for two to three years before they return to land.

Each pair lays a single egg in its burrow. An adult needs about 40 small fish a day, so colonies depend on plenty of small fish nearby.

Main threats

  • Less food: declining stocks of small fish such as sandeels.
  • Climate change, which affects the fish puffins depend on.
  • Introduced predators such as rats, cats and foxes on nesting islands.
  • Oil pollution and drowning in fishing nets.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.

In brief: “Grohnpetrel” started as an invented keyword with no meaning. Our team, the Guillemots, gave it one: a proposed nonprofit initiative for coastal conservation and seabird education, with an original fictional seabird as its mascot.

Where the word came from

In our course Digital Marketing Fundamentals and Search Engine Optimisation at Constructor University, every team competes for search visibility for the same invented term. The lesson behind it is that a keyword does not belong to any topic until someone gives it one. Each team defines its own meaning.

Why we chose a coastal conservation concept

  • A sense of place: Grohn is the Bremen district where our campus lies, and a petrel is a seabird. Together they point to the river, the North Sea and its birds.
  • Useful content: a public-service concept lets us publish sourced information that is useful for readers, not just text written for search engines.
  • Honest framing: we decided from the start that the website must state clearly that Grohnpetrel is fictional, unregistered and not collecting donations.

What search engines said before we started

Before publishing, we recorded how search engines and AI assistants answered “What is Grohnpetrel?”. They treated it as a misspelling of real bird names such as the giant petrel or grey petrel. The full, dated baseline is in our SEO / GEO Lab.

What we published

Our pages now include a definition page, the mission, three proposed programmes, a guide to seabird threats, practical advice, a seabird species guide, an FAQ and this journal. We will report what changes, including what does not work.

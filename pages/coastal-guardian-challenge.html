Can You Protect Our Coasts? Every day, our coastal ecosystems face challenges that affect seabirds and marine wildlife. Test your knowledge, make responsible decisions, and discover how you can help protect our coasts.

5 questions · 3 answers each · about 2–3 minutes · no sign-up, and nothing you choose is saved or sent anywhere.

The five questions

Each question describes a real situation on the coast. Choose the answer you think is right, then open “Show the answer” to check it.

Question 1: Plastic pollution

You find plastic litter near seabirds feeding on a beach. What should you do?

  1. A Leave it because the tide will remove it.
  2. B Collect it if safe, without disturbing wildlife.
  3. C Approach the birds to move them away.
Show the answer

Answer B: Collect it if safe, without disturbing wildlife.

Correct! Removing accessible litter safely can help reduce pollution while allowing seabirds to continue feeding without disturbance.

Learn more: How plastic pollution affects North Sea seabirds

Question 2: Nesting seabirds

You discover seabirds nesting near a coastal walking path. What is the best action?

  1. A Move closer to take a photograph.
  2. B Keep your distance and follow local restrictions.
  3. C Approach the nest to check the eggs.
Show the answer

Answer B: Keep your distance and follow local restrictions.

Correct! Keeping a respectful distance helps reduce disturbance and allows nesting seabirds to care for their eggs and chicks.

Learn more: How to visit the coast responsibly

Question 3: Injured wildlife

You find a seabird that appears injured. What should you do?

  1. A Take it home and try to treat it yourself.
  2. B Give it food and water immediately.
  3. C Keep a safe distance and contact a qualified wildlife rescue service.
Show the answer

Answer C: Keep a safe distance and contact a qualified wildlife rescue service.

Correct! Contacting qualified wildlife professionals is generally the safest response. They can assess the situation and advise whether intervention is necessary.

Learn more: NABU: what to do if you find a wild bird in need (in German, with a map of rescue stations)

Question 4: Coastal dunes

You are exploring a coastal dune ecosystem. Which route should you choose?

  1. A Stay on designated walking paths.
  2. B Walk across the dunes to find a shortcut.
  3. C Walk through protected vegetation to get a better view.
Show the answer

Answer A: Stay on designated walking paths.

Correct! Staying on designated paths helps protect fragile dune vegetation and reduces disturbance to coastal habitats.

Learn more: Coastal habitat protection: dunes, salt marshes and beaches

Question 5: Everyday conservation

Which everyday habit can help reduce marine pollution?

  1. A Leaving small litter behind because it is biodegradable.
  2. B Reducing disposable plastics and disposing of waste responsibly.
  3. C Throwing unwanted items into waterways.
Show the answer

Answer B: Reducing disposable plastics and disposing of waste responsibly.

Correct! Reducing unnecessary disposable plastics and managing waste responsibly can help prevent litter from reaching waterways and marine environments.

About this challenge

The Coastal Guardian Challenge is a short learning game by the Guillemots student team. Each answer comes with an explanation and a link to the page where we cover the topic with sources, such as coastal habitat protection and Take Action. Grohnpetrel is a fictional academic project, not a rescue organisation: if you find an injured bird, contact a wildlife rescue station or a vet.

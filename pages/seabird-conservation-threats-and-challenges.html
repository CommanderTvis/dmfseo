Seabirds play an important role in ocean and coastal ecosystems. Many species spend much of their lives at sea and rely on these environments for feeding, resting, and reproducing.

However, they face various threats, many of which are related to human activities and environmental changes. Among the main issues are pollution, accidental fishing, and the disruption of their natural habitats.

Understanding these threats is key to realizing why it's important to protect seabirds and the places where they live.

Main Threats to Seabirds

1. Plastic Pollution and Marine Waste 

Plastic pollution is one of the problems affecting seabirds. The waste we humans leave behind that ends up in the ocean can be mistaken for food and accidentally eaten by these birds, causing health problems and even death.

In addition, materials like abandoned fishing nets and other debris can get tangled around their bodies, making it hard for them to move and survive.

Read the evidence from the North Sea, with sources: how plastic pollution affects North Sea seabirds.

2. Accidental Fishing and Bycatch

Although it may not seem like it, fishing activities also pose a danger to many seabird species. Some birds approach boats looking for food and end up accidentally getting caught in nets or on hooks.

This problem, known as bycatch or incidental catch, can lead to injuries and drowning. For this reason, it is important to promote fishing methods that reduce the risks for these species.

3. Habitat Destruction and Human Disturbance

Seabirds need safe places to rest and breed. However, building near the coasts, human presence, and other activities can disrupt their habitats. When people get too close to nesting areas, some birds may temporarily leave their nests, leaving their eggs or chicks exposed to various dangers.

4. Oil Spills and Chemical Pollution

Oil spills represent another major danger for seabirds. When oil comes into contact with their feathers, it can damage the natural protection that keeps them dry and maintains their body temperature. This can make it harder for them to swim, fly, and survive, especially in cold waters.

Also, some birds try to clean their feathers with their beaks and end up ingesting toxic substances. Oil pollution not only affects birds directly, but it can also harm other marine organisms they rely on for food.

5. Climate Change and Food Availability

Climate change is also affecting the living conditions of many seabirds. The rise in ocean temperature can change the distribution of fish and other organisms that are part of their diet. As a result, some birds need to travel longer distances to find food, which increases their energy expenditure.

Extreme weather events can also pose a threat. Intense storms and changes in sea conditions can affect breeding areas, destroy nests, and reduce the survival chances of chicks.

6. Invasive Species and Introduced Predators

On some islands and in coastal areas, seabirds have to deal with animals that were not originally part of those ecosystems. Species introduced by human activities, such as rats and cats, can become predators of eggs, chicks, and even adult birds.

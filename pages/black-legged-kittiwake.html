In brief: The black-legged kittiwake (Rissa tridactyla) is a small, ocean-going gull that nests on narrow ledges of sheer sea cliffs, including Helgoland’s bird cliff. It has been classed as Vulnerable since 2017.

Key facts

How it lives

The name comes from the bird’s call, a shrill “kitti-wa-ake”. Kittiwakes build nests on the steepest cliffs, and some colonies have moved onto buildings: on the River Tyne in England, 1,639 pairs nested on buildings in 2020, 879 of them on the Tyne Bridge.

Unlike many gulls, kittiwakes do not feed at rubbish dumps. They depend on small fish near the sea surface, which makes them sensitive to changes in the ocean.

Main threats

  • Fewer sandeels because of fishing and warmer seas; in 2004 a sandeel collapse caused breeding failure in Shetland.
  • Climate change and rising sea temperatures.
  • Bycatch and entanglement in fishing gear.
  • Numbers have fallen by about 40% over three generations since the 1970s.

How you can help

Keep your distance from nesting birds, keep dogs on a lead near colonies, and take your rubbish home. More ideas: Take Action: How to Help Protect Seabirds. Back to the Seabirds of the North Sea guide.
